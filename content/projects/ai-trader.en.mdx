This runs on a **paper account**. There is no real capital at risk, and this page
makes no claim about returns — the interesting part is the machinery, not the P&L.

## The shape of a day

The pipeline runs once, at US market open, in three stages.

**Research.** A screener picks up the most active movers with five-day momentum,
filtered to names priced at or below $50. That price ceiling is not arbitrary: it
keeps positions to whole shares, which is what makes a native broker stop-loss
possible. Candidates are enriched with headlines and sector data.

**Receptivity check.** One decision call goes to Gemini, framed as a value-driven
momentum portfolio manager. Its answer is a proposal, not an instruction.

**Execution.** Whatever survives the gates is entered as a bracket order — a hard
stop-loss at −3% and a take-profit at +10%, both attached at entry.

## The decision that matters

The model does not get the last word. Its proposal passes through risk gating
written in plain code: **$50 per ticker, $250 per sector, $100 per day**. If the
model asks for something outside those bounds, the bounds win.

This inversion is the whole point. An LLM is good at reading a noisy situation and
suggesting something plausible. It is bad at being consistent about risk. So the
part that needs judgement is delegated, and the part that needs discipline is not.

## Idempotency without a database

Each run appends to a per-day journal file, and that same file acts as the
"already traded today" lock. A workflow that fires twice — a retry, a manual
trigger, a scheduling hiccup — finds the day's journal and stops.

No database, no external state store. The artifact that records what happened is
the same artifact that prevents it happening twice.
