## Three stages that can run alone

The pipeline is deliberately not one script. Ticker retrieval, market-return
calculation, CAPM computation and database load are separate modules with files
between them.

The reason is boring and practical: the Finnhub free tier is rate-limited to
roughly one call per second, so a full extraction over the whole US market is
measured in hours. A design where a failure at the load step means refetching
everything is a design that never finishes. Each stage reruns on its own, and
the stage that is slow is the one you never have to repeat.

## CAPM over the whole market, DCF on one name

The cost of equity is computed the textbook way — risk-free rate plus beta times
the equity risk premium — with the market return derived from S&P 500 history
rather than assumed, and with companies whose beta is missing or zero filtered
out instead of silently treated as market-neutral.

That runs across the entire listed universe: **24,426 companies** with a
computed beta and CAPM in the database.

The discounted cash flow side is honest about its scope. Free cash flow is
derived from reported financials as operating cash flow minus capital
expenditure, and it currently runs per ticker rather than across the market —
the extraction of reported statements is far heavier than pulling a single beta.
The arithmetic is there; the scale is not, and this page is not going to pretend
otherwise.

## Checking your own answer

The part worth showing is the second set of scripts, which fetch betas and
historical closing prices through a separate path and compare.

Almost nobody bothers. A pipeline that produces numbers and a report that
displays them will look equally convincing whether the numbers are right or
wrong — the failure mode of a data pipeline is not a crash, it is a plausible
answer. Cross-checking against an independently fetched value is the cheapest
defence available, and it is the difference between a result and a claim.

The database is the seam between the two halves: Power BI reads SQLite and
nothing else, so the report cannot contain a number that the pipeline did not
compute and store.
