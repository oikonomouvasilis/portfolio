Client work, presented anonymously: the brand is not named here, and the
screenshots show layout and typography rather than the mark. The repository is
public but stays unlinked from this page — the specification inside it carries
the name.

## The specification came first

Before any markup, a design brief was written down: palette, type scale, motion,
and eleven rules that are not negotiable. Not a mood board — a document with
prohibitions in it, dated nine days before the first page shipped.

Some of them are ordinary craft. No stock photography from the usual libraries:
if there is no real photograph, the work is done with type, colour or empty
space. No justified text. No fixed-attachment parallax. No heavy drop shadows
under product images, only a contact shadow where the bottle meets the surface.

Some of them are about time. Every animation runs at **800ms or longer**, and
every easing curve is a custom cubic-bezier rather than the browser's defaults.
Fast and bouncy reads as playful, and playful is the opposite of what a bottle
at this price is claiming.

## A rule that is not about design

The hardest line in the document forbids inventing a history the brand does not
have — no generations, no deep roots, no recipe handed down.

It is the easiest paragraph in the world to write and the first thing a reader
stops believing. The rule exists to stop the copy reaching for a past when there
is not one to reach for, and to force the work onto what is actually true and
actually specific: the place, the method, the current harvest, precision.

## Why no framework

Five pages of largely static content, deployed as files. React would have added
a build step, a hydration cost and a dependency tree to a site whose hardest
requirement is that the typography is right.

Tailwind is compiled through PostCSS with a custom config rather than pulled
from a CDN, so what ships is the CSS this site actually uses. The interactive
parts — navigation, a small amount of scroll behaviour — are plain JavaScript,
in files named after the page they belong to.
